About

Welcome to my research website. My work focuses on laboratory plasma experiments, especially magnetic reconnection, lower-hybrid-scale dynamics, electron energization, and diagnostic development. I am also interested in applied plasma science for semiconductor processing, plasma-material interaction, and advanced plasma source design.

Research Areas

Fundamental Plasma Physics

  • Magnetic reconnection and separatrix physics
  • Lower-hybrid drift waves and instabilities
  • Wave-particle interactions and electron energization
  • Electron velocity distribution function measurements

Diagnostics and Applications

  • Thomson scattering and probe-based diagnostics
  • RF, ICP, CCP, and helicon plasma systems
  • Semiconductor plasma processing and etching
  • Experimental system integration and instrumentation

Wave-Driven Electron Energization in Magnetic Reconnection

PHASMA experiment | Magnetic reconnection | Electron kinetics

This project investigates resonant wave-particle interactions during guide-field magnetic reconnection. Using time-resolved plasma measurements, I study how lower-hybrid-scale fluctuations correlate with distortions in the electron velocity distribution function and contribute to kinetic energy conversion.

Advanced Plasma Diagnostics

Thomson scattering | Probe arrays | Instrumentation

I develop and apply advanced diagnostic tools for laboratory plasmas, including Thomson scattering, probe arrays, fast data acquisition, and custom experimental analysis. These tools are used to resolve transient plasma behavior with high temporal and spatial fidelity.

Plasma Processing and Etching Science

ICP/RIE systems | Semiconductor processing | Low-damage etching

I am also interested in plasma processing for semiconductor applications, including fluorocarbon-based etching, process control, and low-damage material removal. This work connects fundamental plasma physics to industrially relevant plasma-surface interactions.
